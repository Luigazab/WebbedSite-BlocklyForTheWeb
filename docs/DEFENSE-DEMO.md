# Defense demonstration and acceptance script

Use an administrator, two teacher accounts and at least two students in separate classrooms. Use demonstration accounts with no real student grades. Record actual results in `RELEASE.md`.

1. Show that public registration offers student accounts. As administrator, create a teacher and show its forced temporary-password change. The account owner enters the new password.
2. Show Overview, Users, Course Templates, Reports and Analytics. Explain the stated time periods and denominators; demonstrate empty/error states where practical.
3. In two classrooms, import the same course. Rename a topic in one. Show that the other classroom and master template retain their original content.
4. As student, join by classroom code. Show a locked topic and its reason, then complete a lecture explicitly.
5. Take a timed randomized quiz. Review its saved attempt, retake it, and show that the best completed attempt supplies the gradebook summary while all attempts remain available.
6. Complete tutorial steps. Save a draft, reopen, validate objectives and finish. Show the saved learning project in My Projects.
7. Read the lab instructions, rubric and passing requirement. Save a draft; submit. Show “Awaiting review,” a blank score and the ability to continue learning.
8. In the classroom Gradebook, preview the immutable submitted output, source and blocks. Enter criterion scores below 75; add feedback. As student, revise and resubmit; as teacher, pass the second revision. Show the first revision's retained score, feedback and files.
9. Show My Progress. Explain why completion/XP do not establish proficiency. Show the checkpoint evidence and pending requirements; use prepared demo records to demonstrate Intermediate/Advanced without awarding fake grades to real students.
10. As teacher, capture a connected stack of supported blocks in one language. Insert the saved composition as student, save/reopen, preview, submit and export. Explain versioning and the deferred custom-input/generator/recursion features.
11. Report a lesson concern with context and an optional screenshot. As administrator, move it through In review and Resolved. Show the reporter's status history and verify another student cannot access it.
12. Sign out. Test recovery with the inbox owner, including expired/used-link behavior, a user-entered replacement password and rejection of the previous password.

## Evaluation record

For each participant use a pseudonymous ID, role, device/browser, assigned tasks, completion result, observed errors, assistance needed and comments. Keep consent and identifying records separately. Use the adviser-approved instrument and sampling procedure. Record technical correctness separately from usability ratings. Do not fill in scores, success rates or conclusions before collecting observations.

## Panel revision mapping

| Revision/decision | Demonstration | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Required fields and readable controls | Account, authoring, rubric and report forms | Browser review; capture defects |
| Guided tutorial character | Tutorial instructions, hints and highlighted blocks | Published three-step sequences; teacher review |
| HTML/CSS/JavaScript coverage | One example and practical task per subject/tier | Curriculum reference and interaction tests; subject approval pending |
| Teacher-added blocks | Captured composition inserted in student work | Reusable version/generation tests; adviser interpretation pending |
| Fair proficiency labels | Checkpoint quiz + reviewed lab, prerequisite tiers | Database classification tests; teacher assessment review |
| Distinct progression and mastery | Pending lab permits continuation; passing review establishes mastery | Submission/revision/idempotence tests |
| Role ownership | Independent imports and denied cross-classroom grading | Database isolation tests; multi-role UI check |
